# Personal statement coaching framework

A strong personal statement answers the reader's main questions without becoming a
technology inventory or a collection of adjectives.

## Building blocks

1. **Professional identity** — Who are you in the context of the target role?
2. **Relevant capability** — What kinds of problems or work can you handle?
3. **Scope and ownership** — How far through the lifecycle can you contribute?
4. **Context or differentiator** — What environments, domains, or combinations of
   strengths shape your value?
5. **Ways of working** — Which supported collaboration, leadership, communication,
   or problem-solving qualities matter for this role?

Not every statement needs all five. Select the strongest relevant evidence.

## Coaching questions

- What role are you targeting, and what professional identity should be clear first?
- Which two or three capabilities are most relevant to that role?
- What can you take ownership of from start to finish?
- What environments or experiences have shaped how you work?
- Which soft skill can be demonstrated through how you deliver, rather than merely
  claimed?
- What should the recruiter remember after reading the paragraph?

## Common problems

- Opening only with `I am...` and then listing technologies
- Leading with years rather than value
- Generic claims such as flexible, passionate, hard-working, or team player
- Mixing every past role into one unfocused paragraph
- Claiming seniority, leadership, or impact without evidence
- Repeating the Skills section

## Suggested drafting scaffold

Sentence 1: professional identity + strongest relevant capability.  
Sentence 2: scope, ownership, or delivery context.  
Sentence 3: differentiating environment/domain/technical strength.  
Sentence 4: relevant way of working or value, grounded in experience.

Use this as a thinking tool, not a mandatory four-sentence template.
