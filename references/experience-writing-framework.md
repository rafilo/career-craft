# Experience writing framework

## What the source images get right

The supplied examples emphasise several useful principles:

- A job description defines a position; a CV should show the candidate's individual
  contribution within that position.
- Passive duty phrases often make candidates sound interchangeable.
- Strong opening verbs improve scanability and clarify ownership.
- Completed outcomes are more informative than time spent on a task.
- Numbers can turn a subjective claim into verifiable evidence.
- Recruiters need to see what changed because of the candidate's work.

## Important correction

Do not mechanically delete every `Responsible for`, `Helped`, `Assisted`,
`Participated`, or `Worked on` and replace it with `Led`, `Built`, or `Delivered`.
That can exaggerate ownership. First discover the candidate's precise contribution.
Sometimes `supported`, `contributed`, `coordinated`, `analysed`, `tested`, or
`documented` is the strongest truthful verb.

Not every bullet requires a number. Use quantified results when verified. Otherwise
use specific scope, artefacts, decisions, adoption, delivery state, quality, risk,
or stakeholder use as evidence.

## ASIE: Action + Scope + Impact + Evidence

### Action

What did the candidate personally do?

Examples: analysed, authored, automated, built, coordinated, designed, delivered,
diagnosed, implemented, migrated, negotiated, optimised, resolved, tested.

Choose verbs based on actual ownership. `Led` is not automatically better than
`analysed`; precision is stronger than status signalling.

### Scope

What was affected or how complex was it?

Possible evidence:

- users, clients, teams, locations, markets, systems, services, releases;
- project phase or duration;
- cross-functional or regulatory context;
- budget, volume, frequency, or operating scale.

### Impact

What changed, improved, became possible, or was protected?

Examples: faster delivery, higher reliability, lower risk, clearer decisions,
greater adoption, reduced manual work, better user experience, successful release.

### Evidence

How can the claim be substantiated?

- verified number, percentage, or before/after comparison;
- delivered, shipped, approved, adopted, or used artefact;
- met deadline/budget/service level;
- defect, incident, risk, or rework reduction;
- stakeholder decision or downstream process enabled.

## The contribution ladder

| Level | What the sentence shows | Coaching response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duty | Job expectation only | Ask what the person personally did |
| Action | Clear personal contribution | Ask about scope or purpose |
| Outcome | A completed result or change | Ask what evidence supports it |
| Evidence | Result with verified scale or proof | Check relevance, concision, and credibility |

This is a diagnostic ladder, not a ranking of the person's worth. A truthful Action
or Outcome bullet is acceptable when metrics are unavailable.

## Coaching questions

Ask only the questions needed for the current bullet:

1. What did you personally do—not merely what the team was responsible for?
2. What problem, goal, or constraint were you addressing?
3. Who or what was affected, and at what scale?
4. What was completed, changed, enabled, or prevented?
5. How do you know it worked?
6. Is there a number, before/after comparison, delivered artefact, adoption signal,
   deadline, quality measure, or stakeholder decision that supports it?

## Examples

Duty only:

> Responsible for quarterly business review presentations.

Questions before rewriting:

- Did you design the review, analyse the data, or only prepare slides?
- Who used it, and what decision or action did it support?

Grounded improvement when the facts are confirmed:

> Designed and delivered quarterly business reviews for senior leaders, translating
> operational data into recommendations used to prioritise improvement initiatives.

Weak participation wording:

> Helped launch a new CRM system.

Questions before rewriting:

- What part did you own: requirements, data migration, testing, training, or rollout?
- What did you produce, and who used it?

Possible grounded improvement when confirmed:

> Authored the functional requirements for the CRM migration, providing a shared
> delivery baseline for the vendor and internal teams.

Weak process wording:

> Worked on improving customer retention.

Questions before rewriting:

- What part of the customer journey did you change?
- What was the observed outcome, and over what period?

Only use a result such as `increased retention by 15% year on year` when the user
provides or verifies it.

## Bullet review rubric

For each bullet, report:

- **Clear action:** yes / partly / no
- **Individual contribution:** yes / partly / no
- **Scope/context:** present / missing / unnecessary
- **Outcome/value:** present / missing / unknown
- **Evidence:** quantified / qualitative / missing / not required
- **Credibility risk:** none / ambiguous ownership / unsupported result
- **Next question:** the single most useful question to ask
