# Editorial style

## Voice

Write with calm confidence. The reader should quickly understand who the candidate
is, what they can handle, and why their experience matters. Prefer precise language
over inflated language.

## Professional summary

- Default to 70–110 words or 3–5 compact sentences.
- Begin with professional identity and strongest relevant capability.
- Connect capabilities to scope, delivery, domain, or business value.
- Include collaboration or leadership only when relevant and supported.
- End with a useful differentiator, not a generic personality claim.
- Do not automatically state years of experience.
- Do not repeat a long technology list already shown in Skills.

## Experience bullets

Use this evidence hierarchy as a diagnostic ladder, not a licence to inflate claims:

1. Action + measurable outcome + context
2. Action + concrete outcome + scope
3. Action + responsibility + purpose
4. Responsibility alone, only when evidence is limited

Teach the user to build bullets with **Action + Scope + Impact + Evidence (ASIE)**.
Not every bullet needs all four elements, but strong bullets normally make the
candidate's personal contribution and its relevance clear.

Good patterns:

- `Improved [system/process] by [action], resulting in [verified outcome].`
- `Designed and delivered [thing] for [users/scope], enabling [outcome].`
- `Collaborated with [people] to [action], improving [quality/speed/clarity].`
- `Owned [lifecycle/scope], from [starting point] through [end state].`

Avoid forcing every bullet into an identical formula. Use 3–6 bullets for recent,
relevant roles and fewer for older or less relevant roles.

`Responsible for`, `Worked on`, `Helped`, `Assisted`, and `Participated in` often
hide the candidate's contribution. Diagnose them, then ask what the person actually
did. Replace them only with a factually accurate action verb. Never upgrade
`participated` to `led`, or `assisted` to `owned`, without evidence.

Evidence does not have to be a percentage. Valid evidence may include scale,
frequency, users, systems, regions, delivery stage, adoption, approval, launch,
quality improvement, risk reduction, time/cost change, or a concrete artefact used
by others. A truthful qualitative outcome is stronger than a fabricated number.

## Skills

- Group by meaningful categories, not one dense comma-separated wall.
- Put the most relevant categories first.
- Use canonical names and consistent capitalisation.
- Do not list soft skills as unsupported keywords when they can be demonstrated.
- Distinguish practical capability from current learning when that distinction
  matters.

## AI wording

State how AI is used in the workflow. Examples of useful specificity include:

- Applying large language models across analysis, implementation, testing, review,
  and documentation.
- Designing agentic workflows and coordinating specialised agents for multi-step
  development tasks.
- Using Claude Code to analyse user stories, support test planning and bug
  investigation, and assist with drafting and refining Playwright automation code.

Use only the elements supported by the candidate's experience.
